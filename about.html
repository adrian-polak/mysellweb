About

Design and code, from one person

Replace with a portrait or workspace photo

I'm a frontend developer and graphic designer, and I care about the same thing from both sides of a screen: does this look right, and does it work.

I started building websites and designing graphics before it became a job title. I'm 19, and I've spent the years since teaching myself both sides of a website: how it looks and how it runs underneath. This isn't the headline of my business. It's the reason I approach a project the way I do.

Most small business websites split into two jobs: a designer who builds the look, then a developer who rebuilds it in code, and detail gets lost along the way. I skip the hand-off. I design in Adobe Illustrator, then build the same layout myself in HTML, CSS and JavaScript, so the site you approve is the site you get.

Illustrator is where most of my graphic work starts, posters, social graphics, logo marks and layouts, before any of it reaches a browser. Vector work stays sharp at every size, from a phone screen to a printed flyer, which matters once a business starts reusing the same graphics everywhere.

A small business rarely needs a big agency. It needs a website and a set of visuals built to look considered, load fast and make it easy for a customer to get in touch. This is the part I focus on: helping small and local businesses present themselves online the way they already present themselves in person.
